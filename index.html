What is TedsWoodworking?

It's a downloadable library of woodworking plans created by Ted McGrath, a woodworker and trainer. Each plan includes step-by-step instructions, a cut list, a materials list and drawings. You access everything from an online members area, and there's an optional physical copy (USB or DVD) for an extra cost.

What's included

  • Buildings & Shelters – sheds, gazebos, playhouses
  • Home Decor and Storage & Organization
  • Workshop & Tools – workbenches, jigs, tool storage
  • Kitchen & Dining, Kids & Recreation, and Animal & Pet (dog houses, birdhouses, chicken coops)

Bonuses listed on the page: a DWG/CAD plan viewer, a set of how-to videos, a "How to Start a Woodworking Business" guide and general woodworking guides. Lifetime members also get new monthly plans and can request custom plans.

  • Hobbyists who want a large library of ideas in one place

Honest pros and cons

Pros

  • Huge range of project types, from small gifts to backyard sheds
  • Plans come with cut lists and materials lists, which make shopping and budgeting easier
  • One-time price, plus a 60-day refund window if it isn't for you
  • Has been on the market for many years

Cons

  • The sales page is very pushy, with countdowns, inflated "total value" numbers and a plan count it doesn't fully explain. Judge the product, not the hype.
  • With this many plans, quality and drawing style vary. Some are simpler or older than others.
  • Plans don't replace practice. You still need basic tools and safety know-how.
  • The physical USB/DVD copy costs extra.

Free alternatives to consider

If you only need one or two simple projects, free plans from hardware-store websites and woodworking YouTube channels may be enough. A paid library makes more sense when you plan to build regularly, or want many designs and exact cut lists in one place.

Bottom line

TedsWoodworking is a big, practical plan library at a one-time price with a 60-day guarantee, so the risk of trying it is low. Ignore the countdown timers and decide based on whether you'll actually build from it. If you're starting woodworking or have a backlog of DIY projects, it's worth a look.
